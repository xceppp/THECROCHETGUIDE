# Article 14 — Yarn Weight Chart

- Slug: `yarn-weight-chart`
- Outside the course · category: charts
- 2 images

Same rule as the hook chart: the numbers belong in the article's table, not in a
generated image. Both prompts forbid printed digits, which also means no yarn
ball bands — a fabricated label with an invented weight number and brand is
exactly the kind of thing that ends up quoted back as fact.

---

## Image 1 — lead

**Save as:** `yarn-weight-chart-1.jpg`
**Goes:** at the top, before the first section
**Alt:** Eight balls of yarn arranged in order from the thinnest thread to the thickest
**Caption:** Illustration. The eight categories, thinnest to thickest.

```
An overhead photograph of eight balls of yarn laid out in a neat row with even
spacing, arranged in order of thickness from the very finest on the left to the
very thickest on the right, so the progression in strand diameter is obvious
across the frame.

The left-hand end is fine thread barely thicker than sewing cotton. The
right-hand end is a very thick chunky roving. Each ball has a short loose strand
curling out from it so the thickness of the individual yarn is visible, not just
the size of the ball.

All the balls are in soft natural undyed tones — cream, oatmeal, pale grey,
warm beige — so the eye reads thickness rather than colour. Every ball is
completely plain with NO paper bands, NO labels, NO printed text, NO digits and
NO branding of any kind.

Shot from directly overhead on a pale oak table, bright soft daylight from the
upper left, soft clean shadows. Bright modern craft photography, sharp focus,
high clarity, natural colour, no plastic sheen. No text, no numerals, no
watermark anywhere in the image. 3:2 horizontal.
```

**Check:** the strands must genuinely range from thread-fine to rope-thick, and no ball may wear a label. This is the image most likely to come back with invented packaging.

## Image 2 — the same stitch at three weights

**Save as:** `yarn-weight-chart-2.jpg`
**Goes:** under `## What each weight is actually good for`
**Alt:** Three crochet swatches of the same size worked in fine, medium and chunky yarn
**Caption:** Illustration. Same stitch, same width, three different yarn weights.

```
An overhead photograph of three square crochet swatches laid side by side with
even spacing, all the same overall width and all worked in the same simple
stitch, in soft natural cream and oatmeal tones.

The LEFT swatch is worked in fine yarn: many small, tightly packed stitches,
producing dense, delicate, almost fabric-like crochet.
The MIDDLE swatch is worked in medium worsted yarn: moderate stitch size, an
everyday familiar density.
The RIGHT swatch is worked in very thick chunky yarn: only a few large chunky
stitches fill the same width, producing bold, open, heavy fabric.

The difference in stitch scale across the three at the same overall width is the
subject of the photograph.

Shot from directly overhead on a pale oak table, bright soft daylight from the
upper left, soft clean shadows. Bright modern craft photography, sharp focus,
high clarity, natural colour, no plastic sheen. No text, no numerals, no
watermark anywhere in the image. 3:2 horizontal.
```

**Check:** all three swatches must be the same width while the stitch counts differ dramatically. If they come out the same scale the comparison is lost.
