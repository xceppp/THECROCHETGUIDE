# Article 08 — Crocheting in the Round

- Slug: `crochet-in-the-round`

This article absorbed the old magic ring page, so it now covers the centre, the
two round methods and the increase behaviour. The four images map to those.

## Image 2 — the hook under both strands

**Save as:** `crochet-in-the-round-2.jpg`
**Goes:** under `## Starting the center: the magic ring`
**Alt:** A crochet hook passing underneath both overlapping strands of a yarn loop
**Caption:** Illustration. Both strands, not one. This is the detail that decides whether the ring closes.

```
An extreme macro overhead photograph of the start of a crochet magic ring,
filling the frame.

A loop of smooth ivory worsted-weight yarn is held open, with the working strand
crossing OVER the loose tail so two distinct parallel strands sit side by side
at the overlap. A plain silver aluminum crochet hook is pushed into the centre
of the loop and is clearly passing UNDERNEATH BOTH of those overlapping strands
at once, lifting them very slightly on its shaft, and hooking the working yarn.

That the hook has caught both strands together is the entire subject of the
photograph and must be unmistakable.

Shot from directly overhead on a pale oak table, bright soft daylight from the
upper left, soft clean shadows. Adult female hands, warm medium-brown skin,
short bare nails, no rings, oatmeal knit sleeves pushed to just below the elbow.
Bright modern craft photography, sharp focus, high clarity, natural colour, no
plastic sheen. No text, no numerals, no watermark anywhere in the image. 3:2
horizontal.
```

**Check:** the single most important image in the whole set. If the hook goes under one strand, the article's central warning is illustrated backwards. Regenerate until both strands are clearly lifted by the hook.
