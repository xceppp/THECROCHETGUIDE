# Article 19 — Halloween Granny Square Variations

- Slug: `halloween-granny-square-variations`
- Halloween set · category: skills · `howTo: true`
- 3 images

Seasonal palette: **pumpkin orange**, **soft white**, **golden yellow**,
**charcoal black** and **deep purple**. Everything else in the house style is
unchanged.

A granny square is a traditional public-domain motif and a worked square is a
swatch, not a project being offered, so a flat finished square is fine here in a
way a finished toy is not. Nothing in this article is presented as a project to
copy.

**Save as:** `halloween-granny-square-variations-1.jpg`
**Goes:** at the top, before the first section
**Alt:** A hook working a cluster of three double crochets into the chain space of an orange granny square
**Caption:** Illustration. Every granny square is the same move: clusters into the gaps, never into the stitches.

```
An extreme macro overhead photograph of a granny square being worked in smooth
pumpkin-orange worsted-weight yarn, filling the frame.

A plain silver aluminum crochet hook is mid-stitch, clearly working a cluster of
three tall double crochet stitches down into an open CHAIN SPACE — the gap
between clusters in the round below — and not into the top of any individual
stitch. The open gap the hook has entered is unmistakable. Two completed
clusters sit either side, separated by their own open spaces, so the square's
characteristic pattern of solid blocks and holes is obvious.

The square is small, only two or three rounds deep, and lies flat.

Shot from directly overhead on a pale oak table, bright soft daylight from the
upper left, soft clean shadows. Adult female hands, warm medium-brown skin,
short bare nails, no rings, oatmeal knit sleeves pushed to just below the elbow.
Bright modern craft photography, sharp focus, high clarity, natural colour, no
plastic sheen. No text, no numerals, no watermark anywhere in the image. 3:2
horizontal.
```

**Check:** the hook must be going into the open gap, not into the top of a stitch. This is the single thing beginners get wrong, so a wrong image here actively teaches the mistake.

## Image 2 — candy-corn rings

**Save as:** `halloween-granny-square-variations-2.jpg`
**Goes:** under `## Variation 1: candy-corn rings`
**Alt:** A flat granny square worked in concentric rounds of yellow, orange and white
**Caption:** Illustration. Candy corn read from the middle out: yellow, orange, then white.

```
An overhead photograph of a single flat granny square filling most of the
frame, lying perfectly flat on a pale oak table, worked in three solid colour
bands that change by round from the centre outward:

- the innermost rounds in golden yellow
- the middle rounds in pumpkin orange
- the outermost rounds in soft white

Each colour occupies complete rounds so the bands form clean concentric squares,
with crisp colour changes at the corners and no blending between bands. The
open gaps between clusters are clearly visible throughout, and the square's
corners are sharp and square rather than rounded or rippling.

Shot from directly overhead, bright soft daylight from the upper left, soft
clean shadows. Bright modern craft photography, sharp focus, high clarity,
natural colour, no plastic sheen. No text, no numerals, no watermark anywhere in
the image. 3:2 horizontal.
```

**Check:** the colour order must run yellow at the centre, then orange, then white at the edge. If the model reverses it, regenerate — the whole point of the variation is that it reads as candy corn.
