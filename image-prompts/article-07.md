## Image 4 — flat, cupped, ruffled

**Save as:** `crochet-increase-decrease-4.jpg`
**Goes:** under `## Keeping a flat circle flat`
**Alt:** Three crochet circles: one lying flat, one cupped like a bowl, one with a ruffled wavy edge
**Caption:** Illustration. Too few increases cups, too many ruffle. Only one of these is a mistake.

```
An overhead photograph of three separate round crochet discs in smooth ivory
worsted-weight yarn, laid in a row with even spacing, each roughly the same
diameter.

LEFT disc: lies perfectly flat against the table, edges completely down.
MIDDLE disc: curves upward at the edges into a shallow bowl or dome, clearly
lifting away from the table.
RIGHT disc: has a wavy, frilled, ruffled outer edge with too much fabric,
rippling up and down around the circumference.

The three different behaviours are the subject of the photograph and must be
clearly distinguishable from each other.

Shot from directly overhead on a pale oak table, bright soft daylight from the
upper left, soft clean shadows. Bright modern craft photography, sharp focus,
high clarity, natural colour, no plastic sheen. No text, no numerals, no
watermark anywhere in the image. 3:2 horizontal.
```

**Check:** all three must look different. This is the hardest image in the set to get right — the cupped and ruffled discs both lift off the table, so if the middle and right look alike, emphasise that the middle is a smooth dome and the right is a wavy frill, and regenerate.
