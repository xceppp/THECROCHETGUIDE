# Article 20 — How to Crochet a Snowflake (and Make It Stay Flat)

- Slug: `how-to-crochet-a-snowflake`
- Christmas set · category: skills · `howTo: true`
- 4 images

Seasonal palette: **white size 10 cotton thread** and a **steel hook**, which is
finer than the aluminum hook in the rest of the set. Everything else in the
house style is unchanged.

White thread on pale oak has the same problem as the ghost article — it blows
out and loses its stitches. Every prompt here asks for directional light and
visible shadow.

All of these are process shots. The blocked flake in image 3 is a technique
sample, not a project being offered.

Image 4 exists because a five-loop attempt at image 2 came back looking like a
flower. Rather than bin it, it now illustrates the counter-example in `## Why
six points`, which is the one place on the page where a five-loop motif is the
correct picture.

## Image 2 — the six spokes

**Save as:** `how-to-crochet-a-snowflake-2.jpg`
**Goes:** under `## The construction, in three rounds`, beside Round 2
**Alt:** A white thread centre with six open chain loops radiating out evenly
**Caption:** Illustration. Six chain loops. Everything decorative hangs off these.

```
An extreme macro overhead photograph of an unfinished crochet snowflake in fine
white cotton thread, lying flat and filling the frame.

It consists of a small tight round centre with SIX open chain loops radiating
outward from it, evenly spaced like the spokes of a wheel, with equal gaps
between them. Place the six loops at the twelve, two, four, six, eight and ten
o'clock positions, so there is a loop pointing straight up and a loop pointing
straight down.

SIX loops. Not five. A five-loop version reads as a flower or a starfish and is
wrong for this image.

Each loop is a clean open arc of chain stitches. Nothing decorative has been
added yet — the piece is a bare six-armed skeleton.

The sixfold symmetry and the even spacing are the subject of the photograph.

Lighting is distinctly directional with clear soft shadows beneath the thread,
so the white keeps its definition against the pale wood.

Shot from directly overhead on a pale oak table, bright soft daylight from the
upper left. Bright modern craft photography, sharp focus, high clarity, natural
colour, no plastic sheen. No text, no numerals, no watermark anywhere in the
image. 3:2 horizontal.
```

**Check:** count the loops before accepting. Exactly six, evenly spaced. The first attempt at this one came back with five, which cannot be used — the section it sits beside says "(6 loops)" and the article states outright that five reads as a starfish. A loop pointing straight up and one pointing straight down is the quickest way to eyeball it.

Lighting is distinctly directional with clear soft shadows beneath the thread,

**Check:** five loops, and it should genuinely look like a flower rather than a failed snowflake. This is the only image in the set where five is correct.
