**Alt:** Two crochet swatches side by side, one worked in short dense stitches and one in tall open stitches
**Caption:** Illustration. Both of these are called "dc" — the left in UK terms, the right in US terms.

```
An overhead photograph of two separate square crochet swatches in smooth ivory

The LEFT swatch is worked entirely in short, dense, tightly packed stitches, so

The RIGHT swatch is worked entirely in tall, open stitches with clearly visible
vertical posts and obvious airy gaps between them.

Both swatches are the same width and the same yarn. The dramatic difference in
density and stitch height between the two is the subject of the photograph.

Shot from directly overhead on a pale oak table, bright soft daylight from the
upper left, soft clean shadows. Bright modern craft photography, sharp focus,
```

**Check:** the two swatches must look obviously unalike. If they read as the same fabric, the image fails to make the point that one abbreviation means two different stitches.
