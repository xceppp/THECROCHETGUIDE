A crochet ghost is one of the friendliest Halloween projects because the silhouette is forgiving. You are not chasing perfect spheres or tiny ears. You need a soft bell shape, a hem that looks a little ragged on purpose, and a face that reads from across the room.

If magic rings still feel shaky, skim [crochet in the round](/crochet-in-the-round) first. This page assumes you can work continuous rounds of single crochet.

## What you need

- White (or cream) worsted-weight yarn — [yarn weight chart](/yarn-weight-chart)
- Small amount of black yarn or embroidery floss for the face
- Hook that matches the yarn ([hook size chart](/crochet-hook-size-conversion-chart))
- Stuffing
- Yarn needle and stitch marker

US abbreviations: see the [abbreviations chart](/crochet-abbreviations-chart).

## The silhouette

Start at the top of the head and work downward. Increase until the head is wide enough, work even for the body, then flare a little at the hem so the ghost looks like it is floating instead of standing in a tube.

You can leave the bottom open and lightly stuffed so the ghost sits over a bottle or candle base (unlit, for decoration), or close it for a soft toy. Both use the same upper construction.

## Head and body

**Rnd 1.** Magic ring, 6 sc. (6)

**Rnd 2.** 2 sc in each st. (12)

**Rnd 3.** *Sc, 2 sc in next st; repeat from * around. (18)

**Rnd 4.** *Sc in next 2 sts, 2 sc in next st; repeat from * around. (24)

**Rnd 5.** *Sc in next 3 sts, 2 sc in next st; repeat from * around. (30)

Work even on 30 stitches until the piece is tall enough that the head-and-body reads as one continuous ghost — usually in the ballpark of the diameter of the widest round, or a bit taller if you want a lankier ghost.

<div class="tip">

**Mark the face height early.** The eyes sit roughly one-third of the way down from the top, not at the equator. A scrap of contrasting yarn tied through a stitch as a temporary marker saves you from embroidering too low after stuffing.

</div>

## Hem: the wavy edge

For a closed toy, decrease back down the way you would for any amigurumi, stuff as you go, and finish. For the classic open ghost:

**Flare round.** *Sc in next 4 sts, 2 sc in next st; repeat from * around (or similar — add six increases once).

**Next round.** Sc around.

**Edging option A — scallops.** *Sc, hdc, dc, hdc, sc in next 5 sts; skip nothing, or skip 1 st between scallops if the edge ruffles too hard.* Adjust by eye. The goal is a soft wave, not a perfect doily.

**Edging option B — simple picots.** *Sc in next 3 sts, ch 2, sl st in second ch from hook; repeat from * around.

Fasten off and weave ends. Stuff the upper two-thirds firmly and leave the hem freer so it drapes.

## Face

With black yarn or floss, embroider two small oval or round eyes using satin stitch or French knots. A tiny curved mouth is optional; many ghosts look better with eyes only.

Do the embroidery **before** the piece is fully closed if you are making a sealed toy — it is easier to hide knots inside. On an open ghost, weave ends into the stuffing cavity.

Avoid glued-on plastic eyes for anything a child might chew. Embroidery is safer and washes better.

## Standing vs flopping

A tall thin ghost with soft stuffing tips over. Three fixes:

1. Stuff the base of the head and upper body denser than the hem.
2. Drop a small sealed pouch of rice or poly pellets into the bottom before finishing (only for shelf décor, not for babies).
3. Widen the increase section so the ghost has a broader footprint.

## Mistakes worth avoiding

**Eyes too low.** Once the hem flares, low eyes make the ghost look surprised for the wrong reasons. Place them high.

**Hem increases every round.** One flare round is usually enough. Stacking increases builds a trumpet that will not sit.

**Black yarn thicker than the body yarn for the face.** Bulky eyes dominate. Use floss or a thinner scrap.

**Skipping the marker on a spiral.** Ghosts are plain fabric; losing the round start shows as a diagonal wobble in the height.

## Frequently asked

### Can I work this in joined rounds instead of a spiral?

Yes. Join and chain 1 at the end of each round if you prefer a clear seam. For a white ghost the seam barely shows; for a spiral, use a marker religiously.

### What yarn color besides white?

Gray, pale blue, or glow-in-the-dark acrylic all work. High-contrast embroidery still matters so the face does not disappear.

### How do I hang it?

Thread a yarn loop through the top before closing, or sew a loop to the magic-ring center. Reinforce with a second pass of the needle so the hanging weight does not stretch a single stitch.
