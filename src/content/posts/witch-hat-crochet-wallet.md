A wallet with one pocket fills up fast, and the card you want is always at the back. This witch hat wallet has an inner divider, so there are two pockets the same size: cards in one, folded cash or a transit pass in the other. The flap closes with a buttonhole you crochet into a row, and the hat sits right above it.

It is one of five patterns in the [Halloween crochet wallet collection](/halloween-crochet-wallets/). The new skills here are a worked buttonhole and seaming through three layers.

The pocket sizes and the 5/8 inch button fit here are worked out from the example gauge rather than measured from a finished wallet, so swatch first and test with a real bank card and your button before you sew up. A standard US bank card is 3.370 by 2.125 inches, the ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 size.

## Materials

- **Worsted weight (CYC 4) yarn** in purple. A cotton or cotton blend keeps the buttonhole from stretching. [Yarn weight chart](/yarn-weight-chart/).
- **Scraps** of black and orange for the hat.
- **US G/6 (4.0 mm) hook**, or the size that gives the gauge. [Hook size chart](/crochet-hook-size-conversion-chart/).
- **One button**, 5/8 in (15 mm), flat, with holes rather than a tall shank. A shank button pushes into the cards.
- Purple thread or yarn for the button, a tapestry needle, stitch markers, one card.

**Estimating yarn.** No sample has been weighed. The rectangle and divider together are about 39 square inches, which is 2.4 four-inch squares. Weigh your swatch, scale it up, and keep a fifth extra for seams.

## Abbreviations and stitches

US terms. See the [abbreviations chart](/crochet-abbreviations-chart/).

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ch | chain |
| sc | single crochet |
| sk | skip |
| sp | space |
| sc2tog | single crochet two together: pull up a loop in each of 2 stitches, yarn over, pull through all 3 loops |
| sc3tog | single crochet three together: the same with 3 stitches |
| st(s) | stitch(es) |

**Turning.** Each row opens with ch 1 and a turn. Do not count that chain.

**Color change.** Work the last stitch of the old color until two loops remain on the hook, then pull the new color through. [Changing yarn color](/changing-yarn-color-crochet/) shows it step by step.

## Size and gauge

**Gauge:** 16 sc and 20 rows = 4 in. Measure a swatch first, as in the [gauge guide](/crochet-gauge/).

Seventeen stitches make 4.25 inches; after the side seams, each pocket is about 3.9 inches wide against a 3.37 inch card. The front and back are 12 rows each, 2.4 inches, so each pocket is about 2.3 inches deep inside, enough for a 2.125 inch card. The flap is 9 rows, about 1.8 inches. The buttonhole is 3 stitches, about 0.75 inch, which is the usual starting point for a 5/8 inch button, since crochet holes stretch.

## The pattern

### Step 1: plain rows up to the buttonhole

![The purple rectangle before it is folded, with the buttonhole worked into the row](../../assets/articles/witch-hat-crochet-wallet-rectangle.jpg "Illustration. In the pattern the hole is centered.")

Use purple.

1. Chain 18. Single crochet in the second chain from the hook and in each chain across. Count 17.
2. For every following row until you reach row 30: chain 1, turn, single crochet in each stitch, count 17.
3. After row 12, put a marker in the last stitch. That is the bottom fold.
4. After row 24, put a marker in the last stitch. That is the top fold.
5. Finish rows 25 through 30 the same plain way. Count 17 at the end of row 30.

### Step 2: the buttonhole, then count back to 17

**Row 31**

1. Chain 1 and turn.
2. Single crochet in the first stitch. Then the next, until you have made 7. Say the numbers out loud.
3. Chain 3. Skip the next 3 stitches. Do not put the hook in them.
4. Single crochet in each of the last 7 stitches.
5. You should have 14 single crochet and one chain-3 space. The hole sits over stitches 8, 9, and 10.

**Row 32**

1. Chain 1 and turn.
2. Single crochet in the first 7 stitches.
3. Make 3 single crochet into the chain-3 space, all three in that same hole. One for each stitch you skipped.
4. Single crochet in the last 7 stitches.
5. Count across. You need 17 again. Sixteen means the space got 2 stitches. Eighteen means it got 4.

Push the button through the hole now, before row 33. It should pass with a small push. If it falls through, pull out rows 31 and 32 and redo them as: row 31 is 7 single crochet, chain 2, skip 2, 8 single crochet; row 32 is 7 single crochet, 2 single crochet in the space, 8 single crochet. If the button will not pass, redo row 31 as 6 single crochet, chain 4, skip 4, 7 single crochet, and row 32 as 7 single crochet, 4 single crochet in the space, 6 single crochet.

**Row 33.** Chain 1, turn. Decrease over the first 2 stitches, single crochet in the next 13, decrease over the last 2. Count 15. Fasten off and weave the tail in. The decrease lets the flap corners lie down.

### Step 3: the divider, one row shorter than the front

<figure class="diagram">
<img src="/diagrams/witch-step-divider.svg" alt="The divider is 17 stitches and 11 rows, one row shorter than the 12-row front, so it hides under the rim." width="480" height="244" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Diagram. Eleven rows, so the divider stays under the rim.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. With purple, chain 18. Single crochet in the second chain from the hook and across. Count 17.
2. Rows 2 through 11: chain 1, turn, single crochet in each stitch, count 17.
3. Stop at row 11. Do not add a twelfth row. Fasten off.

Eleven rows is about 2.2 inches at the example gauge, one row shorter than the 12-row front. The shortfall is what keeps the divider hidden under the rim.

### Step 4: the hat cone, then the brim strip

<figure class="diagram">
<img src="/diagrams/witch-hat-applique.svg" alt="Diagram. The cone in 5 rows: row 1 in orange, 5 stitches, then black, 5, 3, 3, and 1. A separate black brim strip of 8 stitches. On the closed flap, the brim lies on row 30 centered on stitch 9, the tip reaches row 25 at the top fold, and the buttonhole on row 31 sits just below the brim." width="480" height="748" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Diagram. Hat band in orange, the rest in black. Keep row 31 clear.</figcaption>
</figure>

![The small black hat with an orange band, beside a ball of purple yarn](../../assets/articles/witch-hat-crochet-wallet-hat.jpg "Illustration.")

**Cone**

1. With orange, chain 6. Single crochet in the second chain from the hook and in the next 3 chains. That is 4 stitches so far.
2. On the last chain, start the single crochet with orange. When 2 loops are left on the hook, drop orange and pull black through those 2 loops. Count 5.
3. Cut the orange and leave a short tail.
4. Row 2, black: chain 1, turn, single crochet in each of the 5 stitches. Count 5.
5. Row 3: chain 1, turn, decrease, 1 single crochet in the middle stitch, decrease. Count 3.
6. Row 4: chain 1, turn, single crochet in each of the 3 stitches. Count 3.
7. Row 5: chain 1, turn, pull up a loop in each of the 3 stitches, yarn over, pull through all four loops. One stitch remains.
8. Fasten off with a tail about 12 inches long.

**Brim**

1. With black, chain 9.
2. Single crochet in the second chain from the hook and in each chain across. Count 8.
3. Fasten off with a tail about 10 inches long.

At the example gauge the hat is about 1.25 inches wide at the band and about 1.2 inches tall with the brim.

**Sew the hat on before any side seam**, while the flap is one layer.

1. Fold the flap down over the front so you can see where it will land.
2. Pin the brim on row 30, with stitch 9 in the middle of the 8-stitch strip.
3. Pin the cone's orange row on top of the brim. The black tip should reach row 25, at the fold.
4. Whipstitch around the brim and the cone, through the flap only.
5. Look at row 31. The hole must be completely clear. If a stitch covers it, unpick and move the brim up.

### Step 5: fold, sew the divider to the bottom, then seam the sides

<figure class="diagram">
<img src="/diagrams/witch-wallet-divider.svg" alt="Count the side seam upward from the bottom fold. Positions 1 to 11 pass through front, divider, and back. Position 12 is at the top pocket opening and passes through front and back only. The flap stays free." width="480" height="1320" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Diagram. Stitch 1 is at the fold. Stitch 12 is at the opening.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. Fold rows 1 to 12 up against rows 13 to 24, right side out. The chain edge is the front rim.
2. Slide the divider between the front and the back. Its chain edge goes into the bottom fold.
3. Whipstitch all 17 chain stitches of the divider to the inside of that fold. Use shallow stitches so they do not show on the outside. The line has to be continuous. A few tacks leave a gap, and a card will slip under the divider into the other pocket.
4. Clip the three layers at both sides. Divider row ends 1 through 11 line up with the first 11 row ends of the front and back. The front and back have one extra row end at the top. That extra one is position 12.
5. Join purple at the bottom fold on one side. Chain 1.
6. Make single crochet 1 through 11 through all three layers, one in each row end.
7. Make stitch 12 through the front and the back only. The divider has already ended.
8. Count 12. Fasten off. Repeat on the other side.
9. Put one card in each pocket. Neither card should pass under the divider. If one does, the bottom seam has a gap. Open that side and close the gap before you continue.
10. Close the flap. Mark the front through the buttonhole. At the example gauge that mark is near row 7, stitch 9, about 1.3 inches below the fold. Use the mark the buttonhole actually makes.
11. Sew the button there, through the front layer only.
12. Weave the tails along the seams, as in [weaving in ends](/weaving-in-ends-and-blocking/).

Check the continuous divider-bottom seam before closing the sides. Sparse tacks can leave openings between the compartments.

## Make it yours

- **One pocket.** Skip the divider. The seams stay the same.
- **Snap instead of button.** Skip the buttonhole: work rows 31 and 32 as plain sc. Sew a 3/8 inch snap under row 31 at the center and on the front at the mark.
- **Edged flap.** Work one round of slip stitch around the flap edges for a firmer outline.
- **Other hat colors.** A green or purple band, or a black hat with a silver band. Keep the band a color that shows against the flap.
- **Lining.** Line the back only, so the divider still fits. Cut fabric 4.25 by 2.4 inches plus a 1/4 inch fold.

## While you crochet it

**Rows 1 to 30.** Chain 18, single crochet 17, then the same plain row until row 30. Mark the last stitch of row 12 and of row 24 when you make them. Row 12 is the bottom fold. Row 24 is where the flap begins. Losing those marks means guessing the pockets later.

**Row 31, counted out loud.** Chain 1, turn. Single crochet 7. Chain 3. Skip 3 stitches. Single crochet 7. You have 14 single crochet and a hole. The hole covers stitches 8, 9, and 10. If you chained before you finished the 7, the hole sits to one side and the hat will not center over it.

**Row 32.** Single crochet 7, then 3 single crochet into the chain-3 hole, then single crochet 7. Count 17. The 3 stitches replace the 3 you skipped. Two stitches in the hole leaves 16 for the rest of the flap. Four leaves 18. Push the button through now. A little resistance is right. If it falls through, pull rows 31 and 32 and use chain 2, skip 2, with 7 single crochet before the chain and 8 after, and only 2 single crochet in the hole on the return. If the button will not pass, use chain 4, skip 4, with 6 before and 7 after, and 4 single crochet in the hole. Do not keep a hole that only works because you stretched it with the button.

**Row 33.** Decrease, 13 single crochet, decrease. Count 15. This is the row that lets the flap corners lie down. Skip it and the corners stick up.

**The divider.** A separate piece: 17 stitches, 11 rows, then stop. It is one row shorter than the front so it hides. Twelve rows on the divider show a stripe above the rim. Sew its chain edge to the inside of the bottom fold in a continuous whipstitch across all 17 stitches before you seam the sides. Gaps between tacks are holes between the two pockets.

**The hat.** Orange row of 5, changing to black on the last yarn over of that row. Black row of 5. Decrease row to 3. Plain row of 3. Three together, one stitch left. Brim: chain 9, single crochet 8. Fold the flap down so you see the closed wallet, then pin. Brim on row 30, stitch 9 in the middle of the brim. Cone on top of the brim, tip reaching row 25. Whipstitch through the flap only. Row 31 stays clear. A stitch in the buttonhole means the brim is one row too low. Move it. Do not sew the hat through the pocket.

**The side seam, counted from the fold.** Position 1 is the bottom fold. Positions 1 through 11 go through front, divider, and back. Position 12 goes through front and back only, because the divider has ended. Count 12, fasten off, repeat the other side. Put one card in each pocket. A card that slides under the divider means the bottom whipstitch skipped some of the 17. Open the side and close that gap. Then close the flap, mark the button through the hole with cards in, and sew the button through the front layer only.

## Keep the hat above the buttonhole and the divider below the rim

The rectangle divides into front rows 1–12, back rows 13–24 and flap rows 25–33. Fold first to see the closed flap orientation, then pin the hat on the flap only, leaving row 31's hole clear. For a resized buttonhole, use the specified reversed left/right counts on its return row so the extra stitches do not shift the hole. Anchor the divider's entire lower edge inside the fold before seaming the eleven three-layer positions and final two-layer position. Test one card in each compartment separately; neither should pass underneath the divider.

## Buttonhole, divider, and seam as three separate checks

Finish row 32 and put the hook down. Count 17 from the edge that is not the hole, across the 3 stitches in the hole, to the other edge. Then push the button through and take it back out. Only then work row 33. A buttonhole that fails this test gets worse when the flap is seamed, because you can no longer see both sides of the hole at once.

The hat goes on while the flap is still one layer, but only after you have folded the rectangle the way it will close. Otherwise you sew the hat on the wrong face, or with the tip pointing at the buttonhole. Brim on row 30. Tip toward row 25. Hole on row 31 completely outside the brim. Whipstitch the outline and stop. Tug the flap. If the hat shifts over the hole, the stitches were only through the back bump of the yarn. Sew through both loops of the flap's edge stitches.

The divider's chain is sewn to the fold before either side seam. Count those whipstitches: 17, with no bare chain between them. Then the side. Start at the fold so position 1 is the bottom, not the rim. The first 11 stitches go through three layers. You will feel the hook get thicker there. Stitch 12 suddenly goes through two layers. That change is the check. If stitch 12 is still thick, the divider has 12 rows and its top is in the seam. Pull the seam, remove the extra divider row, and seam again. If stitch 11 is already thin, the divider was not caught and one pocket is open at the side.

Cards go in after the seams and before the button. One card on each side of the divider. Close the flap and mark through the hole. The mark on a loaded wallet is the one you sew. An empty wallet sits flatter, and the button ends up too high for the cards you actually carry.

## Common mistakes and fixes

**Row 32 has 16 or 18 stitches.** The ch-3 space got 2 or 4 sc. It takes 3, one for each skipped stitch.

**The buttonhole is off center.** Count 7 before the chain. The hole should be over stitches 8 to 10.

**The flap lifts at the corners.** The decrease row is what lets it lie flat. Make sure row 33 has 15.

**The hat covers the buttonhole.** The brim drifted below row 30. Unpick and move it up a row.

**The divider shows above the rim.** It has 12 rows instead of 11. Pull out the last row.

**The button pulls the front out of shape.** It was sewn without cards inside. Mark with cards in place, and add a small flat button on the inside as a backer if the fabric is soft.

## Care

Wash by hand, cool, and dry it flat. Orange and black scraps sometimes bleed onto purple, so blot a wet scrap of each on paper towel first. Wash it on its own; the button can scratch.

## The color change on the hat, and the three-layer seam

The cone starts in orange. Chain 6, single crochet across until one chain is left, five stitches if you count the last one. On that last stitch, insert the hook, yarn over with orange, pull up a loop. Two loops are on the hook, both orange. Drop the orange. Put the black over the hook and pull the black through both loops. The stitch is black on top and orange underneath. Cut the orange, leave a tail to weave later, and do not carry orange up the side. Row 2 is black in every stitch, chain 1 and turn, count 5. If row 2 has an orange stitch, the color change happened one stitch early. Pull row 2 and the last stitch of row 1.

The side seam is where people drop a layer. Hold the three edges together with a clip at the fold and a clip near the rim. Insert the hook under the row-end of the front, then under the row-end of the divider, then under the row-end of the back, and only then yarn over and finish the single crochet. One stitch, three holes. If you yarn over after two holes, the divider is not in that stitch. Count to 11 this way. On stitch 12, skip the divider on purpose: front hole, back hole, finish the stitch. Fasten off. Do the other side from the fold upward, not from the rim downward. Starting at the rim makes stitch 12 land at the fold, and the divider's top gets sewn into the bottom.

Before the button, open the wallet and look between the pockets. You should see a line of yarn along the fold and no gap a card can slip through. Then load one card each side, close the flap, and mark through the hole you already tested.

## Frequently asked

**Will cards fit both pockets?** On paper, each pocket is about 3.9 by 2.3 inches inside, against a 3.37 by 2.125 inch card, so test both pockets with a real card before you sew up.

**Why 3 stitches for a 5/8 inch button?** Three stitches measure about 0.75 inch at the gauge, and crochet stretches around a button. The test after row 32 is the real check.

**Can I add more pockets?** A second divider makes three, but each pocket then holds only one or two cards, and the side seams become very thick.

**Is the hat sewn or crocheted on?** Sewn. It is crocheted separately so you can position it exactly.

**Can I sell wallets I make from it?** Yes. If you share the design, link to this page; do not republish the rows.

Seventeen stitches at 16 to 4 inches are 4.25 inches wide, and the side seams take a little, which is why each pocket is described as about 3.9 inches inside against a 3.37 inch card. Twelve rows at 20 rows to 4 inches are 2.4 inches, so each pocket is about 2.3 inches deep against a 2.125 inch card. The flap adds 9 rows, about 1.8 inches. The buttonhole is 3 stitches, about 0.75 inch at that gauge, which is the usual start for a 5/8 inch button because the hole stretches. If your swatch is off, the button test after row 32 matters more than the 0.75 inch estimate. Redo the hole, as the pattern says, rather than forcing the button.

The hat's orange row is the band. It has to read as a stripe from arm's length, so do not change to black in the middle of that row. Five orange stitches, then black. The brim is a separate strip of 8 so you can move it. Sewing the cone and the brim on as one piece before you pin them makes it harder to keep row 31 clear. Pin first, sew second, then check that a finger can still find the hole without lifting the brim.
