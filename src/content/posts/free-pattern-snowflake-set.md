## Finished size (design target)

Measured after blocking, point to opposite point.

| Design | In size 10 thread | In worsted yarn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Plain | about 3 in (7.5 cm) | about 6 in (15 cm) |
| 2 — Spiked | about 4 in (10 cm) | about 8 in (20 cm) |
| 3 — Layered | about 4.5 in (11 cm) | about 9 in (23 cm) |

## Gauge

Not critical. Blocking sets the final size, and the same pattern will measure differently depending on how hard you pull the points. Work loosely enough that the holes stay open.

## Materials

- Size 10 crochet cotton thread in white, about 20 yd (18 m) per flake — or worsted cotton for the large version
- Steel hook about 1.65–1.75 mm for thread, or 3.5–4 mm for worsted ([hook size chart](/crochet-hook-size-conversion-chart/) — steel numbering runs backwards)
- Rustproof pins and a foam or cork blocking board
- Stiffener: commercial fabric stiffener, diluted PVA, sugar solution or spray starch
- Yarn needle, wax paper or plastic wrap

## Abbreviations (US)

ch, sc, dc, tr, sl st, st(s), rnd, picot. Full list: [abbreviations chart](/crochet-abbreviations-chart/).

## Notes

- All three are built on six-fold symmetry. Real snowflakes are hexagonal, and six arms read as a snowflake where five reads as a star.
- Weave in ends **before** stiffening. Once the flake is hard the tail sets at whatever angle it dried at.
- Block every flake. Unblocked, all three curl.

## Design 1 — Plain

**Rnd 1.** Magic ring, 12 dc into the ring, sl st to join to the top of the first dc. (12)

**Rnd 2.** Ch 5, skip 1 st, sc in next st. *Ch 5, skip 1 st, sc in next st; rep from * around, sl st into the base of the first ch. (6 loops)

**Rnd 3.** Into each ch-5 loop: 3 sc, ch 3, sl st into the 3rd ch from hook (picot made), 3 sc. Sl st into the next sc between loops. Repeat six times.

Fasten off, weave in, block.

## Design 2 — Spiked

Work Rnds 1 and 2 as Design 1.

**Rnd 3.** Into each ch-5 loop work: 2 sc, then (ch 5, sl st into the 2nd ch from hook, sc in next 3 ch, sl st back into the loop) three times to make three spikes, then 2 sc. Sl st into the next sc between loops. Repeat six times.

Fasten off, weave in, block.

Each arm now ends in three spikes instead of one picot. The arm count is still six, which is what keeps it reading as a snowflake rather than a doily.

## Design 3 — Layered

**Rnd 1.** Magic ring, 12 dc into the ring, sl st to join. (12)

**Rnd 2.** Ch 4, skip 1 st, sc in next st. *Ch 4, skip 1 st, sc in next st; rep from * around, sl st to join. (6 loops)

**Rnd 3.** Sl st into the first ch-4 loop. Into each loop work (3 dc, ch 3, 3 dc), sl st into the next sc. Repeat around. (6 shells)

**Rnd 4.** Sl st across to the first ch-3 space. Into each ch-3 space work: 3 sc, ch 7, sl st into the 4th ch from hook to form a small ring, ch 3, sl st back into the same ch-3 space, 3 sc. Sl st between shells. Repeat around.

Fasten off, weave in, block.

The inner shell round is what gives this one visible depth — two tiers of structure rather than one.

## Blocking, all three

1. Soak until saturated through, not just wet on the surface.
2. Squeeze gently. Do not wring, or the arms set twisted.
3. Lay on wax paper over the board.
4. Pin every point, pulling each arm to full length. Pin the picots and spikes too for crisp tips.
5. Check opposite arms match by eye before walking away.
6. Dry completely — overnight, longer in a humid room.

## Tips

- Work a plain flake and stiffen it before committing to a set. Stiffener behavior varies by brand and by thread.
- Attach the hanging loop **after** stiffening, or it dries flattened into the arm.
- Clear fishing line disappears on a tree; thread does not.
- Store flat between tissue. A flake bent in a bag sets bent.
