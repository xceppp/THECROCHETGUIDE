An ear warmer is a ribbed strip joined into a loop. The starting chain is the width of the band. The rows are the distance around the head. Adding rows does not make it wider. Adding chains does not make it longer. That swap is the whole project.

US terms. US single crochet is UK double crochet. The rib is single crochet in the back loop only. [Abbreviations](/crochet-abbreviations-chart/). The pictures are illustrations. This band has not been worn.

## What is a technique, and what is only a plan

| Chain = width, rows = circumference | The construction. |

| Subtract 2 inches | A planning guess for a stretchy yarn. Not a fit rule. Stiff cotton may not go on if you cut that much. |
| 3.5 stitches and 3.5 rows per inch | One unverified number used for both directions. Row gauge has to be measured on its own. |
| 12 stitches and 70 rows | Arithmetic for about 3.4 inches wide and 20 inches around, and only if both gauges really are 3.5. |
| About 80 yards | An old line. It was not weighed. |

## Step 1: Measure the head, then decide the ease

Tape the path the band will take. Write that number down.

A shorter band than the head is negative ease. It lets a stretchy rib hug. Two inches shorter is the old example: a 22 inch path becomes a 20 inch strip. That 2 inches was not tested on a head. A stiff yarn, or cotton, may need less of a cut, or none. A very elastic wool may tolerate more. The check is the strip around the head before you seam it, not the subtraction.

## Step 2: Swatch both directions

Planning stitch gauge: **3.5 back-loop single crochet = 1 inch.** Work 14 of them. If those 14 are not about 4 inches, change the hook. [Gauge](/crochet-gauge/). [Hook chart](/crochet-hook-size-conversion-chart/).

Then count the rows in one inch of that same swatch. Do not copy 3.5 into the row gauge because the stitches were 3.5. Single crochet rows are often a different number per inch. The old 70-row target is 20 × 3.5, which is right only when the rows really are 3.5 per inch.

Twelve stitches at 3.5 per inch are 12 ÷ 3.5 = about **3.4 inches** wide. That width was not tried on an ear. It is only the paper width of the example.

## Materials

Worsted yarn that will stretch a little after you join it. [Yarn weights](/yarn-weight-chart/). The example hook is a US H/8 (5.0 mm) only if it hits your swatch. Yarn needle, scissors, tape. No weighed yardage.

![Worsted yarn, a hook, and a tape measure](../../assets/articles/how-to-crochet-an-ear-warmer-1.jpg "Illustration. Yardage is not a weighed band.")

## Step 3: Rib until the relaxed strip matches the tape

Chain-1 does not count.

The 12-stitch start is the example width. For a wider band, chain more at the beginning. Chain 16 gives 15 stitches if you still start in the second chain. The circumference math does not change when you do that.

Chain 13.

**Row 1:** Single crochet in the second chain from the hook and across. Chain 1, turn. (12)

**Row 2:** Single crochet in the back loop only across. Chain 1, turn. (12)

![Back-loop single crochet in oatmeal yarn](../../assets/articles/how-to-crochet-an-ear-warmer-2.jpg "Illustration. The rib is the back loop. The strip is still flat.")

Repeat row 2 until the strip, lying relaxed, matches the length you chose in step 1. Do not stretch it up to 70 rows to hit the example. If your row gauge is 3.5 and you did choose 20 inches, 70 rows is that arithmetic. Otherwise stop at the tape.

![The flat band beside a tape measure](../../assets/articles/how-to-crochet-an-ear-warmer-3.jpg "Illustration. Measure the relaxed strip. Do not stretch it to the example.")

Checkpoint: the strip is still 12 stitches wide, or your wider count. A gain at the edge makes one side longer than the other, and the seam will not lie flat.

## Step 4: Seam without a twist

Lay the strip flat. Both short ends should face the same way. Sew through both layers. If one end is flipped, the rib spirals.

![The short ends sewn into a loop](../../assets/articles/how-to-crochet-an-ear-warmer-4.jpg "Illustration. Sew the short ends. Do not twist the band.")

Put the loop on before you weave the ends. This page cannot tell you it will stay put when you look down. It can tell you what to change: if the band is loose, the strip was long; if it will not go on, the strip was short or the ease cut was too deep. Open the seam and add or remove rows. Weaving the ends into the seam, rather than across the ribs, keeps them from reading as a stripe.

A crossed front, where the ends overlap in an X, uses up some length. Measure the loop after you pin the overlap. Do not assume the overlap eats one inch. That inch was a guess.

The seam can sit at the nape so the bump is not on the forehead. That is a comfort preference, not a tested fit.

## Mistakes

**The band is a spiral.** One short end was flipped. Open the seam.

**It will not go over the ears.** The strip is short, or a stiff yarn was cut 2 inches under the head. Use a smaller ease cut, or a yarn that stretches, and measure again before you sew.

**You added rows because it looked narrow.** Rows lengthen the loop. Width is the chain. Start over with more chains if you want more forehead coverage.

**Chunky yarn with 12 stitches and 70 rows.** Those counts are a worsted example, and the 70 already depends on an untested row gauge. Swatch the new yarn in both directions.

## Care

Hand wash cool. Dry flat. Read the label before a dryer. Some wool blends felt, and a felted band does not return to the length you measured.

## Frequently asked

### Will it clear a ponytail?

The band does not cover the crown, so hair gathered on top of the head is out of its path. A low ponytail can sit on the seam. Move the seam, or wear the hair down. This was not tried on a head.

### Is it a first project?

The stitch is a beginner stitch. The first project is still easier as a flat [scarf](/how-to-crochet-a-beginner-scarf/), where a twist does not have to pass over a head. Back loop, if it is new, is in [basic stitches](/basic-crochet-stitches/).
