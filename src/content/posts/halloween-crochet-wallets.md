A Halloween wallet is a quick project, a good gift, and a reason to use up the scraps of black, orange, and purple you bought last October. The trouble with most "wallet" patterns is that nobody checked whether a card goes in. These five were sized on paper against a standard bank card, and each one uses a different construction, so making all five teaches you five techniques rather than one rectangle five times.

![Five Halloween crochet coin purses: a black cat, a ghost, a jack-o'-lantern, a spiderweb, and a witch hat](../../assets/articles/halloween-crochet-wallets-thumb.png)

All five are written for worsted yarn and a US G/6 (4.0 mm) hook. A plain single-crochet swatch checks the gauge the cat, ghost, witch hat, and spiderweb assume. It does not prove a card will fit, and it does not measure the pumpkin, which is worked in the back loop only. Each pattern names the row where you slide a real card in.

Sizes are worked out from a gauge of 16 sc and 20 rows to 4 inches. The pumpkin's back-loop row gauge may differ, which is why that pattern works to a length instead of a fixed row count.

**What you need for all five.** Worsted weight (CYC 4) yarn in black, cream, orange, and purple, scraps of gold, pink, green, and white, a US G/6 (4.0 mm) hook, three 3/8 inch sew-on snaps, two 5/8 inch flat buttons, and a tapestry needle.

## Which one to make first

- **Never made anything but a swatch:** the pumpkin. Straight rows, one fold, two seams.
- **Comfortable with rounds:** the black cat. It has no seams on the pocket at all.
- **Want two pockets:** the witch hat. Cards on one side, cash on the other.
- **Like embroidery more than crochet:** the spiderweb. The crochet is plain; the web is drawn on.
- **Want the most character:** the ghost. It takes longest because of the joining round.

If you need a wallet for a specific number of cards, or want to work out your own size, start with the sizing method in the [Halloween card wallet](/halloween-crochet-card-wallet/) instead. For a smaller, flap-style ghost, see the [ghost mini wallet](/ghost-crochet-mini-wallet/).

Every pattern gives sizes worked out from the example gauge of 16 sc and 20 rows to 4 inches rather than from a finished wallet, so swatch first. A standard US bank card is 3.370 by 2.125 inches, the ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 size, and each pattern tells you where to test the fit with a real card before you commit to the finishing. That test is the one step not to skip.

## Gauge, then a real card

Make a single crochet swatch about 5 inches square on the G/6 hook and measure the middle 4 inches, as in the [gauge guide](/crochet-gauge/). If you get 16 stitches and 20 rows, the cat, ghost, witch hat, and spiderweb are at the gauge those patterns assume. If you get 17 or 18 stitches, go up a hook size. If you get 14 or 15, go down one.

The pumpkin is a different fabric. It is worked in the back loop only, and its row gauge must be measured separately from plain single crochet. Make a second small swatch in back loops, and follow that pattern's instruction to work until the piece measures 4.25 inches.

A matching swatch only means the stitch size is in range. Seams, joins, and the flap still change the opening. Slide a real card in at the step each pattern names before you sew the closure.

Weigh the plain swatch too. Each pattern gives its fabric area as a multiple of a 4 inch square, which is how you estimate yarn without guessing.

## Choose the closure as well as the shape

The collection contains five featured designs, with two additional flap-wallet tutorials linked separately. None of the five is a sealed coin purse. For each project, check its own fabric: the pumpkin uses BLO rows, the cat uses rounds, and the other three use flat rows. All five together require three snaps and two buttons, plus appropriate sewing thread. Make one complete wallet before preparing a batch. Record the hook, measured gauge, actual pocket size, closure position, and card-stack fit so you can reproduce your own result rather than assume the illustration establishes those measurements.

## Mistakes these patterns share

**Skipping the card test.** Every pattern names a point to test. A pocket that is 0.2 inch too narrow will not stretch enough.

**Embroidering in the wrong orientation.** The cat and witch hat flaps are decorated with the flap closed. The ghost face goes on before the panels are joined.

**Sewing closures through both layers.** A snap or button sewn through the whole wallet stitches the pocket shut. Sew through one layer.

**Loose fabric.** Wallets need firmer fabric than a hat or blanket, which is why the hook is smaller than most worsted labels suggest.

## Frequently asked

**Are these patterns tested?** The counts and sizes are worked out from the example gauge rather than from a finished wallet, so each pattern gives you a point to test the fit with a real card as you work.

**Do they hold coins?** None of them is designed for loose coins. They are card sleeves with closures.

**Can I use acrylic yarn?** Yes. Cotton holds the shape better and shows stitch detail, but any worsted yarn that gets the gauge works.

**Can I sell what I make?** Yes, the finished wallets. Please link to the pattern rather than reposting it.

**Where do I learn the basics first?** Start with [basic crochet stitches](/basic-crochet-stitches/) and the [abbreviations chart](/crochet-abbreviations-chart/). Every pattern here uses US terms.

Pick the pattern that matches your skills, swatch the fabric that pattern actually uses, and test a real card where it tells you to. The rest is single crochet.
