Halloween granny squares are ordinary granny squares with a deliberate palette: pumpkin orange, black, and deep purple. The stitch is the same cluster-of-double-crochet motif most beginners learn early. What changes is **when** you switch colors and **how** you keep the joins from looking muddy.

If you have not changed colors cleanly before, read [How to Change Yarn Color in Crochet](/changing-yarn-color-crochet) first. This page assumes that skill and applies it to seasonal squares.

## What you need

- Worsted yarn in orange, black, and purple (scraps are fine)
- Hook matched to the yarn ([hook size chart](/crochet-hook-size-conversion-chart), [yarn weights](/yarn-weight-chart))
- Yarn needle

Abbreviations: [chart](/crochet-abbreviations-chart). Classic granny uses dc clusters and ch spaces.

## Classic granny refresher (one color)

**Rnd 1.** Ch 4, join to form a ring (or magic ring). Ch 3 (counts as dc), 2 dc in ring, ch 2, *3 dc in ring, ch 2; repeat from * twice more, join to top of beginning ch-3. (4 clusters, 4 corner spaces)

**Rnd 2.** Slip to next corner space (or start with a standing dc). *(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in corner, ch 1; repeat from * around, join.

**Later rounds.** Corners always get `(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc)`. Side spaces get `3 dc`. Chain 1 between side groups if your preferred granny style uses them — pick one style and stay consistent so squares match when you join them.

## Variation 1: candy-corn rings

Work each round in a different color: orange center, then purple, then black (or reverse).

Change color on the join: finish the last yarn-over of the joining slip stitch (or the last dc) with the new color, as described in the [color-change tutorial](/changing-yarn-color-crochet). Cut the old color if the next use is several rounds away; carry only if the next round needs it immediately and the carry can hide along an edge you will seam.

<div class="tip">

**Plan the stack before you hook.** Write the round order on a sticky note: R1 orange, R2 black, R3 purple, R4 orange. Guessing mid-square is how you end up with two black rounds stuck together and no orange left for the border.

</div>

## Variation 2: spooky center, quiet border

Keep rounds 1–2 in black, then switch to orange or purple for outer rounds. The dark center reads as a “pupil” or void; the bright outer rounds keep a blanket from looking like a hole punched in fabric.

This variation is forgiving with scrap yarn because the outer rounds use the most yardage and can be whatever Halloween color you have in bulk.

## Variation 3: quartered square (intarsia-ish)

Work a traditional granny, but change color at a corner so two adjacent sides of a round are orange and the other two are black. You will manage two working yarns for a stretch.

Rules that keep this from becoming a knot farm:

- Change in the corner space, not mid-cluster.
- Twist the unused yarn around the working yarn once at the switch so you do not leave a hole.
- Do not carry unused yarn across an open ch space where it shows on the front.

This is the slowest variation. It is also the one that looks most “designed” in a coaster set.

## Variation 4: solid-ish Halloween square

If classic open grannies feel too lacy for coasters, work dc in every stitch and chain-space around, still placing `(2 dc, ch 2, 2 dc)` or `(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc)` in corners. Color-change the same way. You get a denser fabric that sits better under a mug.

## Joining squares into a set

For a coaster set, block each square to the same measurement before joining — see [weaving in ends and blocking](/weaving-in-ends-and-blocking). Join with slip stitch or whip stitch in black so the seams disappear into the darkest color.

For a small wall hanging, join three squares in a column with purple joins and add a black border round around the whole piece.

## Mistakes that muddy Halloween colorwork

**Changing color at the start of a round after the chain-up.** The chain stays the old color and leaves a blot. Change on the last yarn-over of the previous stitch or join.

**Too many colors in one small square.** Three is plenty. Five scrap colors read as clutter, not festive.

**Tight chain spaces in black yarn.** Black hides mistakes and also hides when you are crocheting too tight. Check drape in daylight.

**Skipping end-weaving because “it is only a coaster.”** Black ends that poke through orange show forever. Weave in as you go.

## Frequently asked

### Do I need glow-in-the-dark yarn?

No. It can be fun as a one-round accent. As the main fabric it is often scratchy and weak. Use it sparingly if at all.

### Can I mix acrylic and cotton in one square?

You can for practice. For a set you care about, keep fiber content consistent so blocking and wash behave the same.

### What size should a coaster be?

Large enough for the base of your mug plus a little margin. Measure the mug, not a guess from memory. Make one square, set the mug on it, then decide whether to add a round.
