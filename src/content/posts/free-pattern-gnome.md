## Finished size (design target)

Sizes assume the gauge below, weighted and stuffed, measured to the tip of the hat with the hat sitting naturally. Re-measure on your sample before publishing.

| Version | Target height | Target base width |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small | about 5 in (13 cm) | about 3 in (7.5 cm) |
| Tall | about 8 in (20 cm) | about 4 in (10 cm) |

## Gauge

14 sc and 16 rounds = 4 in (10 cm) in continuous sc with the yarn and hook below. A firm fabric that does not show stuffing matters more than hitting the number exactly.

## Materials

- Worsted weight acrylic ([yarn weight chart](/yarn-weight-chart)): body color (A), hat color (B), skin tone (C)
  - Small: about 50 yd (45 m) A, 40 yd (37 m) B, 5 yd (4.5 m) C
  - Tall: about 110 yd (100 m) A, 90 yd (82 m) B, 8 yd (7 m) C
- Fuzzy pale yarn for the beard (D), about 15 yd (14 m)
- US G/6 (4.0 mm) hook, or size needed for a firm fabric ([hook size chart](/crochet-hook-size-conversion-chart))
- Stuffing
- Dry rice or pie weights, in a small fabric bag
- Yarn needle, stitch marker

## Abbreviations (US)

sc, sc2tog, rnd, st(s), sl st, ch, invdec (optional invisible decrease). Full list: [abbreviations chart](/crochet-abbreviations-chart).

## Notes

- Work in a spiral unless noted. Mark the start of every round.
- The weight bag goes in **before** any stuffing, sitting flat on the base.
- Do not stuff the hat.
- Offset increases by a stitch or two between rounds so they do not stack into a seam.

## Pattern — Small body (A)

**Rnd 1.** Magic ring, 6 sc. (6)

**Rnd 2.** 2 sc in each st. (12)

**Rnd 3.** *Sc in next st, 2 sc in next st; rep from * around. (18)

**Rnd 4.** *Sc in next 2 sts, 2 sc in next st; rep from * around. (24)

**Rnd 5.** *Sc in next 3 sts, 2 sc in next st; rep from * around. (30)

**Rnds 6–7.** Sc in each st around. (30)

**Rnd 8.** *Sc in next 4 sts, 2 sc in next st; rep from * around. (36)

**Rnds 9–10.** Sc in each st around. (36)

**Rnd 11.** *Sc in next 5 sts, 2 sc in next st; rep from * around. (42)

**Rnds 12–17.** Sc in each st around. (42)

Insert the weight bag, then stuff above it. Fasten off leaving a long tail and flatten the opening closed with a whip stitch across the top.

## Pattern — Tall body (A)

Work as Small through Rnd 11. (42)

**Rnds 12–14.** Sc in each st around. (42)

**Rnd 15.** *Sc in next 6 sts, 2 sc in next st; rep from * around. (48)

**Rnds 16–25.** Sc in each st around. (48)

Weight, stuff, and close as for Small.

## Hat (B) — both sizes

**Rnd 1.** Magic ring, 4 sc. (4)

**Rnds 2–8 (small) or 2–12 (tall).** Sc in each st around. (4)

This is the floppy tip. Longer reads as more deliberate.

**Next rnd.** 2 sc in each st around. (8)

**Following rnds.** Work 2 rounds even, then one round increasing 6 sts evenly, repeating until the opening is slightly wider than the body at the point where the hat will sit — about 44 sts for Small, 50 for Tall.

Work 2 rounds even. Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing. Do not stuff.

## Nose (C) — both sizes

**Rnd 1.** Magic ring, 6 sc. (6)

**Rnd 2.** 2 sc in each st. (12)

**Rnds 3–4.** Sc in each st around. (12)

**Rnd 5.** Sc2tog around, stuffing lightly. (6)

Fasten off leaving a long tail. For the Tall gnome, add one more even round before decreasing.

## Beard (D)

Cut strands roughly twice the finished beard length. Fold each in half, pull the loop through a stitch on the body front with the hook, then pull the ends through the loop and tighten.

Work an arc of these across the upper front of the body — about 14 strands for Small, 20 for Tall. Trim straight or to a point.

## Assembly

1. Beard first, in an arc across the upper front.
2. Nose centered, directly above the top of the beard, covering the attachment line.
3. Hat last. Pin it so the brim touches the top of the nose, check from a few feet away, then sew around the brim.

## Tips

- If the hat perches instead of sitting down, it is too narrow. Add one more increase round.
- A gnome that tips forward usually has the weight bag sitting toward the back.
- Use a bag for the weight. Loose rice migrates out through the stitches over a couple of seasons.
