A spiderweb on a crochet wallet usually means a lot of fiddly white color changes. This one skips that. You crochet a plain black wallet with a pointed flap, then draw the web on top with backstitch, which you can pull out and redo until it looks right. A tiny crocheted spider sits on one strand.

It is one of five patterns in the [Halloween crochet wallet collection](/halloween-crochet-wallets/), and the one that teaches surface embroidery and a button loop.

The pocket size, flap drop, and loop length here are worked out from the example gauge rather than measured from a finished wallet, so swatch first and slip a real bank card in before you join. A standard US bank card is 3.370 by 2.125 inches, the ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 size.

## Materials

- **Worsted weight (CYC 4) yarn** in black. Smooth cotton makes the white embroidery look crisp; fuzzy yarn blurs it. [Yarn weight chart](/yarn-weight-chart/).
- **White** worsted or a thinner white yarn, or six-strand embroidery floss, for the web.
- **Purple** scraps for the spider.
- **US G/6 (4.0 mm) hook** for the wallet, and a size smaller for the spider if you have one. [Hook size chart](/crochet-hook-size-conversion-chart/).
- **One button**, 5/8 in (15 mm), flat with holes.
- Tapestry needle, a sharp embroidery needle if using floss, chalk or a white pencil, stitch markers, one card.

**Yardage.** There is no weighed sample to quote. The wallet covers about 26 square inches, so weigh a 4 inch square swatch and allow 1.6 times that weight, then a fifth more for seams and the spider.

## Abbreviations and stitches

All terms are American; the [abbreviations chart](/crochet-abbreviations-chart/) lists the UK equivalents.

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ch | chain |
| sc | single crochet |
| sl st | slip stitch |
| sc2tog | decrease: draw up a loop in each of the next 2 stitches, then yarn over and pull through every loop on the hook |
| sc3tog | as sc2tog, over 3 stitches, removing 2 |
| inc | 2 sc in the same stitch |
| MR | magic ring; see [crochet in the round](/crochet-in-the-round/) |
| rnd | round |
| st(s) | stitch(es) |

**Turning.** Start each row with ch 1 and turn; that chain is never counted, and the first sc goes into the very first stitch.

**Backstitch.** Bring the needle up one stitch length ahead, go down at the end of the previous stitch, and repeat. It makes a solid line that can follow any direction.

## Size and gauge

**Gauge:** 16 sc and 20 rows to 4 inches. Measure the middle of a 5 inch swatch before starting; the [gauge guide](/crochet-gauge/) explains why the edges lie.

Seventeen stitches are 4.25 inches; after the side seams, the pocket is about 3.9 inches wide inside against a 3.37 inch card. The front and back are 13 rows, 2.6 inches, so the pocket is about 2.5 inches deep, enough for a 2.125 inch card with room to grip it. The flap is 8 rows, about 1.6 inches to the point, and the loop hangs about 0.4 inch past it.

## The pattern

### Step 1: the straight rectangle

A chain-8 loop sits at the point." width="480" height="652" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

![The black pointed flap before any embroidery](../../assets/articles/spiderweb-crochet-wallet-flap.jpg "Illustration.")

Use black.

1. Chain 18. Do not count the loop on the hook.
2. Single crochet in the second chain from the hook, then in every chain across. Count 17.
3. For each new row: chain 1, turn, single crochet in the first stitch and in every stitch across, count 17.
4. Keep a tally. After row 13, put a marker in the last stitch. That marker is the bottom fold.
5. Keep going the same way through row 26. Put a second marker in the last stitch of row 26. That is the top fold.
6. Stop the plain rows there. You have one rectangle, 17 stitches wide and 26 rows tall.

### Step 2: the point, one decrease row at a time

<figure class="diagram">
<img src="/diagrams/spider-step-point.svg" alt="Rows 27 to 34 each lose one stitch at both ends: 15, 13, 11, 9, 7, 5, 3, then 1." width="480" height="244" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Diagram. Both ends decrease on every flap row.</figcaption>
</figure>

A decrease uses two stitches to make one. Pull up a loop in the first stitch, pull up a loop in the next, yarn over, pull through all three loops.

1. **Row 27.** Chain 1, turn. Decrease over the first 2 stitches. Single crochet in the next 13, counting them. Decrease over the last 2. Count the row: 15.
2. **Row 28.** Chain 1, turn. Decrease, single crochet in the next 11, decrease. Count: 13.
3. **Row 29.** Decrease, 9 single crochet, decrease. Count: 11.
4. **Row 30.** Decrease, 7 single crochet, decrease. Count: 9.
5. **Row 31.** Decrease, 5 single crochet, decrease. Count: 7.
6. **Row 32.** Decrease, 3 single crochet, decrease. Count: 5.
7. **Row 33.** Decrease, 1 single crochet in the middle stitch, decrease. Count: 3.
8. **Row 34.** Chain 1, turn. Pull up a loop in each of the 3 stitches, yarn over, pull through all four loops. One stitch is left.

If any row is an even number, a decrease was missed at one end. Pull that row out. The list is 15, 13, 11, 9, 7, 5, 3, 1.

### Step 3: the button loop

<figure class="diagram">
<img src="/diagrams/spider-step-loop.svg" alt="Chain from the point and slip stitch back into the same stitch. The button should pass with a little push." width="480" height="244" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Diagram. The loop returns to the same stitch at the point.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. Chain 8 from the one stitch at the point.
2. Slip stitch back into that same stitch, so the chains form a loop.
3. Hold your button against the loop. It should pass with a little push, not fall through.
4. If it falls through, pull the loop out and chain 7. If it will not pass, chain 9 or 10. Stay inside 7 to 10 chains.
5. Fasten off and weave the tail into the point.

<div class="tip">

The flap edges will be a little bumpy where each row decreases. A round of slip stitch along both slanted edges, worked loosely, smooths them. Do it before the embroidery.

</div>

### Step 4: mark the five spoke ends

<figure class="diagram">
<img src="/diagrams/spiderweb-wallet-web.svg" alt="Diagram. The closed flap with five white spokes running from the point at row 34 to row 27 at stitches 2, 5, 8, 11, and 14, and three rings at rows 29, 31, and 33, each made of four segments that dip toward the point. A purple spider sits between the rings at rows 29 and 31 on the fourth spoke." width="480" height="684" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Diagram. Spokes first, inner ring first, spider last.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. Fold rows 1 to 13 up. Fold the flap down over them. You are looking at the flap the way it will sit when the wallet is closed.
2. On row 27, count stitches from the same edge every time. Put a pin in stitch 2, stitch 5, stitch 8, stitch 11, and stitch 14.
3. Stitch 8 is the middle. The middle spoke will run straight from the point to that pin.

**Spokes.** Thread white. Start at the point, row 34. Backstitch a straight line to the pin at stitch 2: come up one stitch-length ahead, go down where the last stitch ended, repeat. Then a line from the point to stitch 5, then stitch 8, then stitch 11, then stitch 14. Five lines, all starting at the same point. Go through the flap only.

**Inner ring.** At row 33, stitch from the first spoke over to the second, then second to third, third to fourth, fourth to fifth. That is 4 segments. Leave each one a little long and nudge it toward the point with the needle so it bows. A tight stitch makes a straight bar and cups the flap.

**Middle ring.** The same 4 segments at row 31.

**Outer ring.** The same 4 segments at row 29.

On the back of the flap, run the white tail under the strands you already stitched. Do not knot it. The back of the flap rubs the front of the wallet. If a line is crooked, unpick that line only. Backstitch pulls out cleanly.

### Step 5: the spider body, one round at a time

<figure class="diagram">
<img src="/diagrams/spider-applique.svg" alt="Diagram. Left, the abdomen: magic ring of 6, round 2 increases to 12, round 3 even, round 4 decreases to 6. The head: magic ring of 6 and one even round. Right, the head sewn to the abdomen with eight two-segment legs, four per side, bent at the knee." width="480" height="716" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Diagram. Two tiny balls and eight stitched legs.</figcaption>
</figure>

![The purple spider on the right side of the web, with the loop and button at the point](../../assets/articles/spiderweb-crochet-wallet-spider.jpg "Illustration. The pattern uses five spokes.")

Use purple. A hook one size smaller than the wallet hook makes a tighter ball. If you do not have one, use the same hook and keep the stitches snug.

**Abdomen**

1. Make a magic ring. If the ring is new, the [in the round](/crochet-in-the-round/) page shows the loop. Single crochet 6 into the ring. Count 6. Pull the tail so the hole closes.
2. Round 2: 2 single crochet in each of the 6 stitches. Count 12.
3. Round 3: 1 single crochet in each stitch. Count 12. Do not increase.
4. Round 4: decrease 6 times, once over every pair of stitches. Count 6.
5. Push a scrap of purple yarn into the hole.
6. Fasten off. Thread the tail through the front loop of each of the 6 stitches and pull until the hole closes. Keep the tail for sewing.

**Head**

1. Magic ring, 6 single crochet. Count 6.
2. One more round of 1 single crochet in each stitch. Count 6.
3. Fasten off and close the 6 stitches the same way. It should cup into a bead, not a disk.

**Sew it on**

1. Sew the head to one end of the abdomen with the abdomen tail.
2. Find the spoke that ends at stitch 11. That is the fourth spoke if you count from stitch 2.
3. Sew the spider onto that spoke, between the ring at row 29 and the ring at row 31. Tack the head and the back of the abdomen. One tack lets it spin.
4. Legs: 4 on each side, 8 in all. Each leg is two straight stitches. The first goes out from the body. The second is shorter and bends toward the point.
5. Before the legs, the spider is about 0.5 inch across.

### Step 6: fold, seam the sides, then mark the button

<figure class="diagram">
<img src="/diagrams/spiderweb-wallet-closure.svg" alt="The flap ends in a chain-8 loop. With cards inside, close the flap without stretching it and mark where the loop lands on the front wall. Remove the cards and sew the button through the front wall only." width="480" height="1240" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Diagram. Mark the loop on the closed flap, then sew the button through the front wall only.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. Fold between row 13 and row 14, right side out. Pair front row 13 with back row 14, row 12 with row 15, and keep going until front row 1 meets back row 26. Row 1 is now the top front rim, not the bottom.
2. The flap, rows 27 to 34, stays free. Do not seam it.
3. Join black at the bottom fold on one side. Chain 1.
4. Make 1 single crochet through both layers in each of the 13 paired row ends. Count them. Fasten off.
5. Do the other side the same way: 13 single crochet, then fasten off.
6. Put in the cards you mean to carry. Lower the flap without stretching it.
7. Mark the front wall where the loop actually lands. Ignore any guessed row number if the fit is different.
8. Take the cards out. Sew the button at that mark, through the front wall only. If the needle comes out inside the pocket, you have stitched the pocket shut.
9. Weave the tails along the seams. See [weaving in ends](/weaving-in-ends-and-blocking/).

## Make it yours

- **Purple wallet, black web.** Reverse the colors. Black backstitch on purple shows less, so use two strands.
- **More spokes.** Seven spokes at stitches 1, 3, 5, 8, 11, 13, 15 look closer to a real web but crowd near the point. Stop the outer ones at row 31.
- **No spider.** Embroider a small one instead: a French knot body and eight straight-stitch legs.
- **Snap closure.** Skip the loop. Sew a 3/8 inch snap under the flap point and on the front.
- **Lining.** Line the back only, so the pocket keeps its width. Cut fabric 4.25 by 2.6 inches plus a 1/4 inch fold.

## While you crochet it

**The rectangle.** Chain 18, single crochet 17. Then chain 1, turn, single crochet 17, for every row through row 26. Mark the last stitch of row 13 and the last stitch of row 26 as you pass them. Those marks are the folds. If you mark them later from memory, the pocket and the flap will be different depths. Count the side ridges if you lose the tally: 26 ridges, two marks, at 13 and 26.

**The point.** Each flap row decreases at both ends and nowhere else. Say the count before you turn: 15, 13, 11, 9, 7, 5, 3. Row 34 is the three remaining stitches worked together, one stitch left. An even number means one end was plain single crochet. Pull that row. The edges look bumpy where each row lost a stitch. A loose slip-stitch round along both slanted edges, done before the web, smooths them. A tight slip stitch pulls the point into a cup, so if the edge draws in, pull the slip stitches out and leave the bumps.

**The loop.** From the one stitch, chain 8 and slip stitch back into that same stitch. Hold the button in the loop. It should need a small push. If it falls through, redo with 7 chains. If it will not enter, redo with 9 or 10. Do not leave a loop that only fits the button before the flap is folded. Yarn loops stretch. Test again after the side seams, with cards in.

**The web, on the closed flap.** Fold the front up and the flap down before you mark anything, so the point is at the bottom of what you are looking at, the way it will be worn. On row 27, pin stitches 2, 5, 8, 11, and 14, counting from the same edge. Backstitch from the point to each pin. Five lines, one start. Then the rings, inner first: at row 33, one stitch from spoke to spoke, four segments, each left long enough to bow toward the point. Then row 31, then row 29. A tight ring looks like a straight bar and cups the flap. Unpick a crooked line. Backstitch comes out clean. Secure the white by running it under the stitches on the back of the flap. No knot. That back rubs the wallet.

**The spider.** Abdomen: magic ring of 6, increase to 12, one plain round of 12, six decreases back to 6, a scrap of yarn inside, then close by running the tail through the six front loops. Head: magic ring of 6, one plain round of 6, close the same way. Sew head to abdomen. Sew both to the spoke that ends at stitch 11, between the rings at rows 29 and 31, with a tack at the head and a tack at the back of the abdomen. Eight legs, four a side, each leg two straight stitches, the second one bent toward the point.

**The seams and the button.** Fold between rows 13 and 14, right side out. Pair row 13 with row 14, row 12 with row 15, down to row 1 with row 26. The chain edge is now the front rim. The flap stays free. Join at the bottom fold and work 13 single crochet up the side through both layers, one per paired row end. Repeat on the other side. Put the cards in. Lower the flap without pulling it. Mark where the loop lands. Take the cards out. Sew the button through the front wall only. If the thread shows inside the pocket, the needle went too deep and the pocket is partly sewn shut.

## Pair the folded pocket rows before marking the button

Fold between rows 13 and 14. Pair front row 13 with back row 14, row 12 with 15, continuing to front row 1 with back row 26. Row 1 is now the top front rim, not the bottom. Sew only those thirteen row-end pairs on each side. The flap starts at row 27 and ends at row 34, so it remains free. With cards inside, lower the flap and mark the loop's actual landing place; ignore any estimated front-row number if your fit differs. Embroider only after fixing the flap's closed orientation, and keep the needle out of the pocket.

## The flap counts, one row at a sitting

Do not start a decrease row until you have said the target out loud. Row 27 uses the 17 stitches of row 26 as decrease, 13 single crochet, decrease, and you must be holding 15. Row 28 uses those 15 as decrease, 11, decrease, and you are holding 13. Row 29: decrease, 9, decrease, holding 11. Row 30: decrease, 7, decrease, holding 9. Row 31: decrease, 5, decrease, holding 7. Row 32: decrease, 3, decrease, holding 5. Row 33: decrease, 1 single crochet, decrease, holding 3. Row 34: one decrease across all 3, holding 1. The loop is chained from that 1 and slip stitched back into it.

When you backstitch the spokes, finish one line and look at it before you start the next. The line should sit on top of the flap, not pull the point toward the pin. If the point curls after a spoke, that spoke is tight. Unpick it and restitch it looser before you add the next spoke. Five tight spokes will not relax when the rings go in. The rings are only as flat as the spokes under them.

Place the spider only after all three rings are in. The fourth spoke, the one at stitch 11, is the perch, and only the part of it that lies between row 29 and row 31. Sewing it on the point covers the loop. Sewing it on row 27 puts it on the fold, where the flap bends, and the legs catch the pocket.

## Common mistakes and fixes

**The flap is off center.** One decrease row missed a decrease at one end. Count each row: 15, 13, 11, 9, 7, 5, 3, 1.

**The spokes pull the flap into a cup.** The backstitch was too tight. Keep each stitch the length of one crochet stitch and do not tug.

**The rings look like straight lines.** They need a little slack. Leave each segment slightly long, then nudge it toward the point with the needle.

**The white strand shows gray.** Black fiber traveled with the needle. Use a sharp needle and go into the center of each stitch.

**The loop is too loose.** Crochet loops stretch. Redo with one chain fewer.

**The spider wobbles.** It is sewn only at one point. Tack it at both the head and the back of the abdomen.

## Care

Wash by hand in cool water and lay it flat to dry, spider up. Before the first wash, press a damp scrap of the black yarn on a white paper towel; if color transfers, the web will gray.

## Backstitch on the black flap

Cut a length of white you can manage, about an arm's length. A longer strand picks up black fiber and comes out gray. Thread a sharp needle if you are using floss. A blunt tapestry needle is right for the yarn, but it will snag if you try to split the black stitches. Come up at the point, where row 34 is. Go down one stitch-length along the path to the first pin, at stitch 2 on row 27. Come up one stitch-length farther along that same path, then go down in the hole you just left. That overlap is the backstitch. Each stitch should be about as long as one single crochet on the flap. Shorter stitches pile up and pull the point. Longer stitches gap, and the spoke looks dashed.

Finish the spoke at the pin. Do not carry the white across the back to the next pin in a long straight pull. That pull is what cups the flap. End the strand, or leave it loose on the back and start the next spoke at the point again. Five separate starts at the point look like one center. One strand dragged between the ends of the spokes draws a second web on the back and shows through at the edge.

For a ring segment, come up on one spoke at the row you are on, and go down on the next spoke at that same row, leaving the white a little longer than the gap. With the needle tip, push the middle of that segment toward the point until it bows. Then backstitch along that bowed line in short stitches so it stays. Do the inner ring, at row 33, before the middle ring, at row 31, before the outer ring, at row 29. An outer ring stitched first has nothing to sit outside of, and the spacing goes uneven.

When the white is done, turn the flap. The back should show short white dashes, not loops. Run the tails under those dashes. Clip them close. A tail left long will felt into the black and make a pale smear on the front of the wallet.

## Frequently asked

**Will cards fit?** On paper, the pocket is about 3.9 by 2.5 inches inside against a 3.37 by 2.125 inch card, so test the fit with a real card before you join.

**Can I crochet the web instead of embroidering it?** Possible, with surface slip stitch, but it is harder to control on a small triangle. Backstitch can be undone.

**Is the spider safe for a child?** It is sewn on, but small parts on any handmade item can come loose. For a young child, embroider the spider instead.

**Why a loop, not a buttonhole?** A buttonhole cannot go into the last few stitches of a point. The loop does the same job at the tip.

**May I sell wallets made from this?** Go ahead. Share a link to this page instead of copying the instructions.

Seventeen stitches at 16 to 4 inches are 4.25 inches. After the side seams the pocket is about 3.9 inches inside, against a 3.37 inch card. Thirteen rows at 20 rows to 4 inches are 2.6 inches for the front and 2.6 for the back, so the pocket depth is about 2.5 inches against a 2.125 inch card. The flap is 8 decrease rows, about 1.6 inches to the point at that gauge, and the loop hangs a bit past the point. None of that has been measured on a finished wallet. Put the card in before you seam. If it bends, the swatch was tighter than 16 stitches to 4 inches and the fix is a larger hook from row 1.

Sew the button with the same doubled thread you would use for a snap: through each hole, through the front wall, through a small extra stitch on the inside of the front only if the yarn is soft, and never through the back. The inside stitch is a backer, not a second button. It keeps the button from tearing the front. It must not catch the back panel, or the pocket will not open.
