# Article revision findings

The collection contains 64 published articles and 10 drafts, as verified against the uploaded files. It is a mix of full project patterns, techniques, reference guides, and idea roundups. Not every article should be turned into a full pattern: the two idea collections explicitly write out only the heart or star and link to other tutorials.

## What this pass does not establish

1. **Physical pattern success.** The object has not been made from the revised text. The counted oval can still fail footprint fit; a sound decrease sequence can still make the wrong silhouette. All wearable sizes, pocket clearances, yarn quantities and completion times need real observations.
2. **Complete visual agreement.** Eight original assets were sampled visually, not every stage image. The supplied bowl image resembles a round/boxed basket rather than proving square-and-dart construction; the tote image appears to have a boxed base although the tutorial is a flat two-panel bag. The AirPods concept image also includes details not defined by the plain sleeve method. These photos remain unchanged. New exact diagrams improve construction guidance but do not turn concept photos into sample photographs.
3. **Every calculation checked by a parser.** Thirty-three focused arithmetic checks cover selected critical constructions and repeated shaping families. This is not an exhaustive executable crochet validator. Seam placement, fabric behavior and resizing remain human/sample checks.
4. **Full live-site integration.** The export is partial source, not a runnable project. Build, responsive rendering, diagram display, progress anchors and production links still need checks in the actual repository. Do not blindly overwrite later edits.
5. **Draft readiness.** The ten drafts were retained unchanged and must not be presented as audited published patterns.
6. **External-source completeness.** Primary Craft Yarn Council hook, yarn-weight and abbreviation sources were consulted, and Apple device-spec pages and AAP safe-sleep guidance were retrieved. Access rules and catalog descriptions on the pattern-database roundup were not reverified for every publisher.

## Physical sample workflow

Start with the traffic-facing AirPods and five featured wallets, then the hood, beanie, gloves and slippers. Give a tester the revised instructions without additional verbal explanation. Ask them to record every point where they had to guess, exact yarn and hook, actual gauge, part dimensions, finished size, closure behavior and yarn used. Photograph the actual foundation, each shaped part, pre-assembly orientation, join insertion and final opening.

For the ghost, specifically test the 17-stitch scalloped join, card clearance below the snap, and 19-stitch plain-hem variation. For AirPods, test both oval axes, snugness, lid movement and flap reach using an actual case. For the divider wallet, check cards cannot cross compartments beneath the divider. For the hood, verify crown, face and neck edges on a wearer before reporting an adult size. For bags and bottle sleeves, do not infer a carrying load rating from a sewing diagram.

Use tester evidence to correct the files, replace mismatching concept images with real stage photographs, and only then report that a particular pattern was physically tested. Preserve failures and fixes in internal revision notes; the reader needs the final usable instruction, not the editorial history.
