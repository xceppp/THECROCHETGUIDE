# Premium article template

Derived from the bowl-cozy and hooded-scarf rebuild. Use it when a project page is too thin to teach. Do not use it to lengthen a page that is already clear.

The three reference books named in older notes are not in this workspace. Nothing here is copied from them. Stitch facts used on the two pages are the ordinary US definitions and the Craft Yarn Council head-circumference chart.

## What to teach

1. Name the skill, separate from the object. The cozy teaches a square, a join rate, and a dart. The hood teaches a fold. The yarn and the hook are not the lesson.
2. Split established technique from numbers that were never made. Put that split in one table near the top.
3. Give the construction in the order a person actually decides things: measure, swatch, stitch count, fabric, then assembly.
4. Say why a step exists in the same place as the step. The join rate exists because 5 rows and 4 stitches do not fill an inch the same way.
5. Put a checkpoint where a mistake becomes expensive: stitch count every 10 rows, side length before the pinch, hood on the head before it is sewn to the scarf.
6. Show one worked example with the arithmetic visible. If the example fails a published body measurement, keep the example as the failure. Do not replace it with a new untested size.
7. Add a diagram only when it shows a relationship the paragraph would otherwise leave ambiguous: which edge is the opening, which stitches the side should have, what a pinch removes.
8. Say what is still untested, once, in the place it changes a decision. Do not repeat that sentence in every paragraph.
9. If the page cannot tell a reader to make the object, say so, and say whether the URL should stay unpublished until someone makes it.

## What not to do

- Do not add a paragraph whose only job is to raise the word count.
- Do not convert an unweighed yardage into a shopping number.
- Do not draw a new "safe" measurement to hide a failed one.
- Do not use a concept illustration as evidence that the object was made.
- Do not drop a heat warning, a melt warning, or a "not worn" limit in order to sound more confident.

## Page shape

- First sentences: what the object is made of, and which decision matters.
- Warning, if a real harm or a known sizing failure is in play.
- Table: technique versus plan.
- Steps a person can follow without inventing a measurement.
- One comparison table when two numbers need to be seen together.
- Mistakes, written as symptom then cause then fix.
- Care, if the fiber needs it.
- Questions a person actually asks after the steps, not restatements of the introduction.
- Two or three links to the skill page that repairs the mistake.

## Diagram rule

An SVG diagram is warranted when the reader has to see which edge, which count, or which subtraction is which. Reuse a stitch diagram that already exists. Label the diagram as a sketch when the fabric has not been made. Match the site ink, paper, terracotta, and sage. No fake finished-object photos.

## Bowl cozy and hooded scarf

### Informational change

The old cozy page had the square formula, the 48 by 60 rows, a 2 inch pinch, and a heat warning. It did not require the base measurement, did not show why one stitch per row end ripples, and did not show that a 2 inch pinch is already shorter than a 3 inch wall while also leaving an 8 inch center that may be the wrong size for the base.

The new cozy page keeps every heat and fiber warning. It adds the base as a third measurement, the stitch arithmetic, the 4-for-5 join, the pinch subtraction, and checkpoints. It drops 300 yards as a shopping figure because that number was never weighed.

The old hood page gave chain 38 and about 30 rows, then said the folded opening was about 6 inches and too small. The 6 inch figure mixed the depth with the opening. The opening of that panel is the width, about 12 inches. The new page separates those two, checks 12 inches against the Craft Yarn Council head chart, and refuses to replace 12 with a made-up adult width. The scarf can still be planned from a tape. The hood cannot be planned without a hood that already fits. The page tells the owner not to publish the chain-38 hood as a wearable pattern.

### Still needs a physical test

Bowl cozy:

- The 4 sc and 5 rows gauge.
- Whether a square at rim + wall + wall, after a real dart, clears the rim and sits on the base.
- The 4-for-5 join in yarn, including whether the corner's 3 stitches sit cleanly.
- Any heat behavior. None is claimed. Do not test this by microwaving it.
- Yardage.

Hooded scarf:

- The 3 hdc and 2.5 row gauge.
- Any hood opening, depth, and crown seam on a person.
- Whether sewing the open edge to one scarf edge sits at the neck instead of the mouth.
- Yardage.
- The chain-38 panel. It should not be made as the pattern. It is the counterexample.

Recommendation: keep the hood URL as an explanation only after review. Do not put the old wearable instructions back. Set the page aside from publication as a project pattern until a hood has been measured, made, and worn. The cozy can stay a method page, with the heat warning, because the size is taken from the bowl in front of the reader and the failed 2 inch pinch is no longer an instruction.
